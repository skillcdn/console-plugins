The widgets lay themselves out for the room their frame has, whatever size and place the front page gives them: a console that composes the page edge to edge, or a person who arranges it, hands a widget boxes of any shape, which the name of a size did not describe. Every widget is now a lead, a body and a foot, side by side when the box is wide, one under the other when it is square or tall, and the lead alone when it is small. The body keeps the rows it has room for, each whole, and says how many more there are; nothing is cut and nothing scrolls. The size a widget is told no longer changes what it shows.
