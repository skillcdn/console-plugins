# Roadmap

What exists, what is being built, what comes next. Update this file in the same change that starts, finishes or drops an item; the organization's own tracking of the work stays on its board, never here. One plugin at a time, each written as the console's skill `building-a-plugin` writes one: the design first, shown to the person; then the code and its tests; the pages looked at on a console of this machine; the release; and the install handed to the deployment as a task.

## Open points of `hr`

Decided with the person before they are built; none blocks the first version.

- Leave in units smaller than a quarter day (Korea's rule from 2027-06-10 lets a worker take leave by the hour, up to five days a year under the draft decree) and hours for part-time schedules: hours on the request, as a unit beside quarters.
- Attendance's rate of 80%, which decides the year's 15 days in Korea: known once attendance is a feature; until then HR adjusts.
- Sick leave accrued by hours worked, as US state laws have it: the same dependency on attendance.
- A blackout span nobody takes leave in, a file on a request (a medical certificate; the `files` capability), a document on the board at year end with the figures for payroll.
- The settlement at termination compared with the hire-date basis, which Korea's fiscal-year practice asks for: a query that shows both counts, HR still writing the adjustment.
- The records' quota (100,000 per plugin) against the ledger: a line per grant, use and expiry per person per year is small; attendance is the feature that will decide between a day as one record and `storage:sql`.

## Open points of `messenger`

Decided with the person at its design checkpoint; none changes the decisions above.

- A slash command (`/휴가`) beside the shortcut: only if a spike on a real workspace finds that typing the name after `/` does not bring the shortcut up, or that a name in Korean is refused. The shortcut is the entry because its name is a label in any language, it needs no scope of its own, and it collides with no other app's command.
- To be measured on a real workspace, on the first console Slack can reach that runs the plugin (a console on this machine cannot be reached; its address is its own loopback): whether a customized name and picture survive an edit of the message; whether a manifest update that adds a shortcut needs the app reinstalled; the seconds a form takes to open against the three a messenger allows.
- A route that takes the request at submission, for an organization that wants requests in a channel as well as the thread; the thread is the default.
- A workspace of one messenger per console; several (an enterprise's) at a later design checkpoint.
- What a person does through the chat is a member's: the console has a plugin acting for a person stand as a member, whatever they administer, and the role of a manager follows the reporting line and is granted to nobody, so the form requests the presser's own leave alone; recording leave for one's people, and an answer by someone above the decider or by a member of the board project, stay on the board. A form for a manager's acts waits for a need.
- The role `messenger` asks of `hr` when `hr` is enabled after it: the console offers a grant when enabling a plugin and lists what it asks on the plugin's page, and does not ask again when the producer appears; the setup page says what is missing and points there. Asking again was proposed and not built; proposed again only if administrators miss it.
- A route narrowed to a team of the console's (a team's leave to its channel), since the directory names each person's teams: at the design checkpoint.
- A messenger that signs with RS256 or wants a webhook address per channel asks the console for more; proposed, low.

- Installing a plugin otherwise than as a release of a deployment: no store here, nothing installed at runtime.
