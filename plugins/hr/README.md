# HR

The people's area of work, as a plugin of the SkillCDN Console. Its first feature is **leave**: a person, or their agent, requests days off; their manager in the console decides on the board project, with whatever steps an organization adds; the balance comes from a policy HR chose, written for Korea's statutory rules by hire date or by fiscal year, for two common shapes of a US policy, for a flat number of days, or kept by hand; every change of a balance is a line of a ledger that is never edited; HR enrolls the people whose leave is managed here, one by one or all, and decides who sees whose balance and who is out. **Announcements** stand beside it: what HR tells everyone, written with a toolbar, with pictures, on a page of their own and on the front page. Attendance and the people's records come next, in the same plugin, under the same roles.

The contract is `docs/specs/plugins.md` of the console's repository, shipped in `node_modules/@skillcdn/console/docs/specs/plugins.md`; what this repository asks on top is [docs/guide/conventions.md](../../docs/guide/conventions.md).

## What the plugin is not

A plugin is no labour lawyer. The presets say what the law said when they were written (the Korean ones, Labor Standards Act articles 60 and 61 as of 2026, with the amendment of 2026 that takes effect in 2027 noted below), and every figure they produce is HR's to check against the law of the day; where the two differ, HR corrects the balance with `adjust` and a note. The plugin counts as it was told to, shows every count with the line that produced it, and leaves every decision to a person.

## The design

The table a plugin is read against: a row per thing the feature does, who starts it, what it became and the test that proves it ([building-a-plugin](https://github.com/skillcdn/console/blob/main/skills/building-a-plugin/SKILL.md), phase 1). The tests are `src/server/index.test.ts` (the commands, the queries, the hook and the job against the console's test context), `src/server/announcements.test.ts` (the announcements), `src/server/leave/approval.test.ts` (the line of a request), `src/server/leave/rules.test.ts` (the periods, the tiers, the credits each policy owes and what is in time), `src/server/leave/balance.test.ts` (the settlement of a ledger), `src/server/leave/holidays.test.ts` (the calendars) and `src/server/skills.test.ts` (every command line the skills and this page name, against the definitions).

**How a balance is counted.** Every credit (a grant, an accrual, days carried over, a positive adjustment) is a bucket with the last day it may be used; every use takes from the bucket expiring soonest that is valid on the leave's first day; a cancelled leave's `use` and its `reverse` cancel each other; an expiry or a payout closes the bucket it names. What is available on a day is the buckets valid that day less what was taken and what waits. A request for a period to come is checked against that period's grant before it is written, under a policy that gives the period's days upfront. The ledger is append-only: a mistake is corrected by another line, and the versions of the other records keep who changed what. The settlement is `src/server/leave/balance.ts`, and the rules of periods, tenure, tiers and credits are `src/server/leave/rules.ts`, both pure and both tested on their own.

**A policy, field by field.** `basis` (periods from the hire anniversary, or from a fixed month and day), `earning` (the period's days are for the year served before it, as Korea has it, or for the year ahead), `firstPeriod` (what the first fixed period start before the first anniversary grants: prorated by the days served or ahead, the base in full, or nothing), `firstYearMonthly` with `monthlyUntil` and `monthlyMax` (one day per completed month in the first year, usable until the first anniversary or the period's end), `tiers` (completed years → days), `tenureAt` (the tier read at the period's start or its end, the latter giving the extra day in the year the anniversary falls), `accrual` (upfront at the period's start, monthly, semimonthly, every two weeks, or by hand), `cap` (the balance an accrual stops at), `carryOver` with `carryOverDays` and `carryOverMonths` (nothing, everything, or up to a number, usable for some months), `advanceDays` (how far below zero a person may go), `countDays` (working days, or every day), `rounding` (a prorated figure exact, or up to the half or the whole day), `reminderDays` (days before expiry HR is reminded, as a task) and `payoutUnused` (unused days at expiry recorded as a payout owed or as expired). The presets (`src/server/leave/presets.ts`) are `kr-statutory`, `kr-fiscal-year`, `us-accrual`, `us-frontloaded`, `fixed` and `manual`; `console plugin hr presets` prints every field each sets.

**What the law said, as the presets have it.** Korea: 15 days after a year with 80% attendance, one day per full month in the first year up to 11, one more every two years from the third year up to 25 (art. 60); the first year's days lapse at the first anniversary (since 2020-03-31); the year's days arise the day after the year is completed, so a person who leaves on the anniversary's eve gets the first year's monthly days alone (Supreme Court 2021다227100; the Ministry's reading since 2021-12-16), which `creditsDue` keeps; the use-promotion notices six and two months before expiry (art. 61), which the reminders time; unused days owed as pay unless promoted. The fiscal-year basis is practice, not statute: it must leave no one worse off than the hire-date basis, and the difference is settled at termination, by HR. An amendment promulgated 2026-06-09 (Act 21784) lets a worker take leave in units of time from 2027-06-10, half days at least and up to five days a year under the draft decree: the kinds here take half and quarter days where HR allows it, and hours are a later feature. Workplaces under five people are outside article 60 as of 2026; the attendance rate the plugin cannot know is HR's to apply by adjustment. United States: no federal vacation mandate; state sick-leave laws accrue by hours worked, which attendance will bring; California and like states forbid use-it-or-lose-it and owe unused vacation at termination, so `us-accrual` carries everything over under a cap. The public holidays: Korea's as the Regulation on Public Holidays has them in 2026, with Labour Day and Constitution Day since 2026-05-01 and the substitute-holiday rules, the lunar dates from a table of years the code names (2025 to 2028; another year is refused until added); the US federal ones from their rules, observed on the nearest weekday.

### Roles

| Role | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| `member` (everyone, the default) | every member of the workspace | requests and cancels their own leave; reads their own balance, requests and ledger; reads the calendar, the holidays and the announcements |
| `hr` | granted to HR | everything: the people, the policies, the kinds, the holidays, every balance, every request, the adjustments, the export, and writing the announcements |

An administrator holds every role. Being a manager takes no role: the console says who reports to whom, on its People page, and whoever stands above a person holds what their nearest manager holds, at any depth, as an administrator does over everyone. So the pages `approvals` and `people` and the widget `approvals` are every member's, and say so where a person manages nobody. A manager must be able to work in the board project, since a decision is addressed within it. Teams are the console's to keep: the plugin holds none, so that a calendar or an exception by team waits for them. The words are 매니저 for the manager, as the console has it, 결재선 for the line, and 결재 for the act; 승인 and 반려 stay the answers.

### Board, settings, menu

The board project is required: the decisions and the tasks go there; the defaults make a project `hr` (HR / 인사), private as the console makes one. The console lets anyone who works in it answer a decision, so it holds those who approve and nobody else; a person outside it waits on `requests`, since the console does not show them the decision. The manifest asks an administrator nothing else: what an organization decides about leave is HR's, not an administrator's, and the console's settings page is an administrator's alone, so it is the record `config`, on the Setup page and with `configure`: nothing secret and no capability is in it, every write is a command of the role `hr`, a `plugin.command` event on the board and a version of the record. Menu group `people`; pages `overview`, `announcements`, `leave`, `calendar`, `approvals` and `people` (members, wide; the last two say so where a person manages nobody) and `setup` (HR, wide); widgets `announcements` (small, medium, large, wide), `balance` (small, medium, large), `today` (small, medium, large, wide) and `approvals` (small, medium, large), which a person arranges on the front page.

```sh
pnpm turbo run build --filter @skillcdn/console-plugin-hr     # dist/server/index.js and dist/web/
pnpm turbo run test --filter @skillcdn/console-plugin-hr      # the rules, the settlement, the calendars, the module against the test context, and the package as a console reads it
pnpm --filter @skillcdn/console-plugin-hr run check           # console-plugin check: what a console, or a store, would refuse
```

To look at it on a console of this machine, `pnpm plugins-dir` writes the directory a console reads its plugins from, with this plugin in it; [docs/guide/local-console.md](../../docs/guide/local-console.md) says how the console is started with it and the plugin enabled.

## Make it yours

Most of what an organization wants is a policy, a preset and the fields it overrides, and a line with its exceptions, on the Setup page or with `console plugin hr policy` and `configure`. A rule no field expresses is a change to `src/server/leave/rules.ts` or `approval.ts`, proven in the tests beside them; a country's calendar is a generator in `src/server/leave/holidays.ts`; a preset is an entry of `src/server/leave/presets.ts` with its `about` saying what law or practice it follows; a kind of leave a workspace should start with is a line of `src/server/leave/types.ts`. A fork publishes under its own scope, as [docs/guide/releasing.md](../../docs/guide/releasing.md) says.

## Enabling it

What the administrator does, which the hand-over task quotes:

1. Consent to the capabilities above, each read as its sentence.
2. The board project: make `hr` from the defaults, or choose one. Every manager must be able to work in it, a member or an administrator; a decision the console refuses goes on up the person's managers, to the administrators and to HR, with a task to mend it, and to the administrator who enabled the plugin where there is nobody else.
3. The roles: `hr` to the people who administer leave (`console plugin hr grant hr --person <login>`); `member` is everyone's. Being a manager takes no role: each person's manager is set on the console's People page.
4. HR opens the Setup page: three questions (the country, who approves, who sees what) set leave up, and every answer can be changed afterwards. `console plugin hr setup kr` and `configure` do the same.
5. HR enrolls the people on the People page, or as the skill `hr-administration` says: `console plugin hr enroll <login> --leave on --hired-on <day>` for each person whose leave is managed here. Only enrolled people with leave on are counted; the rest see the pages and no balance.
6. The skills: `console plugin hr skills` once it runs; the rules an agent reads name the plugin.

## What the deployment must provide

Nothing beyond the console: no `storage:sql`, no outbound host; the pictures of announcements are the console's files, kept where it keeps every file. The manifest's `console` says the consoles it runs on, `>=0.4.4 <0.5.0` ([ADR-0006](../../docs/adr/0006-a-plugin-has-a-version-of-its-own-and-declares-the-consoles-it-runs-on.md)): 0.4.4 brought what it stands on, a decision addressed to several people, a person's language and manager in the directory, who was granted a role, their pictures, a widget's sizes, and the workspace's week.

## The pages

Each page has a head, its title and a line beside it with its action at the right, and each section is a pane of its own, in the console's own look: its glass panes, view switches, avatars, progress bars and empty states. A widget lays itself out for the size it is told, and the announcements show as many rows as their tile has room for, each whole, since a tile's height follows the width of the page. The Setup page draws a line as it is walked, from the request to the approval, each step saying in words who it asks, so that a second step of `manager` reads as the manager's manager. The server module decides everything: a page shows what the queries answer, and the form that requests leave asks `preview` what the request would come to.

The editor is ProseMirror, in a file of its own that only opening it loads, so that a widget and a reader carry none of it: `src/web/editor/schema.ts` is what it can hold, which is what `src/shared/rich-text.ts` can say, and `src/web/pages/announcement-editor.ts` the toolbar around it. What is pasted is read in a document that runs nothing, keeps the look a style gave it as tags, and goes through the schema, which drops what it does not name. The pages read a text with no editor at all: `src/web/rich-text.ts` builds it element by element.
