# Messenger

The plugin of the SkillCDN Console that tells people, where they already are, what the console's plugins have for them, and brings back what they do there ([ADR-0008](../../docs/adr/0008-every-notification-leaves-the-console-through-the-messenger-plugin-which-reads-what-it-tells-as-a-principal.md)). Slack first, an adapter inside the plugin. For HR: one shortcut in Slack, 휴가 (Time off), whose form requests leave and cancels the person's upcoming leave, and each request threaded with the person and whoever decides, approved or rejected with a button that answers the console's decision for whoever pressed it; approved leave told to the channels an organization chooses, in the person's name ([ADR-0009](../../docs/adr/0009-a-message-about-a-persons-own-action-is-shown-as-theirs-under-the-apps-mark-and-as-the-plugins-where-nobody-matches.md)), struck through when it is cancelled. One app, installed in each Slack workspace that takes it, a console holding the projects of several teams each in a workspace of its own ([ADR-0014](../../docs/adr/0014-several-slack-workspaces-are-one-app-installed-in-each-and-a-project-is-told-in-the-workspace-it-is-for.md)). And what the console tells each member of, sent on to them in a direct message: the console decides who is told of a task given, ready, moved, changed, commented or put away, a decision waiting or answered and an agent account standing otherwise, and the messenger sends that on to whom it told, each choosing which of it reaches them in Slack ([ADR-0017](../../docs/adr/0017-the-console-decides-who-is-told-and-the-messenger-sends-on-what-it-told-of-which-each-member-chooses-what-reaches-them.md)). Whoever holds the role `messenger` keeps it; everyone chooses what reaches them on a page of their own or through their agent, and otherwise meets it in Slack.

The contract is `docs/specs/plugins.md` of the console's repository, shipped in `node_modules/@skillcdn/console/docs/specs/plugins.md`; what this repository asks on top is [docs/guide/conventions.md](../../docs/guide/conventions.md).

A delivery is at least once: each hook keeps the event's id in `deliveries` and passes over one it has told, and reads the thread before it writes, so that a step asked or a channel told is not asked or told again. Slack asking to wait is told to the console as `retryAfterMs`, so that the hook's next try waits that long. A decision's address is never written where someone it does not reach reads it, since a group conversation holds the requester too and a channel holds everyone: the buttons answer, and the link is to each reader's own decisions on the console.

**Collections** (data version 4). The console's record routes answer them to an administrator, and a member's own choice and what waits for them to that member; whoever keeps the messenger reads the queries ("Who reads what" below). `apps`, one record: the app's id and client id, the language of the names the app shows, the app's name and its bot's as chosen, the icon chosen, the one Slack took last and why Slack did not take it, whether its manifest holds the shortcut and that manifest's digest, the installation links good now (state, until when, who asked), the last problem, when the configuration token was renewed. `connections`, one per workspace the app was installed in: the workspace's id and name, the bot user and its handle, `pending` until accepted, `connected` or `removed`, whether it is the default, the projects told there by the console's ids, who gave out the link and who installed it in Slack, its last problem, its last matching's counts; indexed by workspace and state. `routes`: the kind, the workspace, the channel's id and name, the language, `person` or `app`; indexed by kind, channel and workspace. `links`: a person's account in a workspace, the name it goes by there and its picture's address; owned by the person, indexed by person, member and workspace, no history. `threads`: what was written about one thing a producer told, the workspace and the conversation, the request's message, each step with the decision it asks and its messages each in its workspace, the channel posts; indexed by thing, state and workspace, no history, forgotten 400 days after its last change. `deliveries`: a line per event the messenger had someone to send to, or one that follows an agent account, sent, skipped or failed with what happened and the id of what it was about, and, while one that failed part way is tried again, whom it was sent on to already, so that the retry tells nobody twice; indexed by event id, time and outcome, no history, forgotten after 30 days. `preferences`: what a member chose of what reaches them in Slack, what of the console's notifications is not sent on (its kinds by its names, and `followed`), at once or daily and when, the day the last day's message went, and nothing of who is told, which is the console's; one per person, theirs and read by them alone, indexed by person and cadence, no history. `waiting`: a line of a person's message of the day, the kind it was told as, its sentence in their language and its address, and what happened in parts no language is in (the thing, who did it, the states, the fields, what was said, the moment an agent account's usage limit resets), by which the day's message puts a thing's lines together, optional so that a line kept by 0.4 is read as it is; theirs and read by them alone; indexed by person and time, no history, forgotten after 3 days. `silences`: an agent account the console does not hear from, since when, when that was told and when it was heard from again, and whom the console told of it, who are whom the job tells, put away once that is told or nothing is left to tell; indexed by the account, no history, forgotten 30 days after its last change. `limits`: an agent account its usage limit stopped, as it was said: since when, the moment it was said to reset, when the account ran again and whom the console told of that, who are whom the job tells, put away once its return is told or it stops otherwise; indexed by the account, no history, forgotten 30 days after its last change.

**Roles.** `messenger` (Messenger / 메신저 담당), granted by an administrator, who holds it as every role: the connection, the routes, the people and what was sent. `member` (Member / 구성원), the `defaultRole` every member holds: My Slack notifications, `notify`, `notices` and `project-notices`, each about the caller alone. What people do in Slack reaches the console as theirs through the console's call for a person.

**Capabilities**, each as the administrator reads it:

| Capability | What the messenger does with it |
|---|---|
| `people:read` | Names people in messages and on its pages. |
| `people:read:all` | Reads everyone from a job or a hook, and hears when someone joins, leaves, comes back or has their email changed, to keep each person's account matched. |
| `people:read:email` | Reads each person's work email to match them to their Slack account; an address goes to Slack alone, to look one person up. |
| `people:act` | Acts for a person who pressed something in Slack: requests or cancels their own leave in HR, and answers a decision on their plate, recorded as theirs through the messenger. |
| `projects:read` | Names the projects on its pages, to say which Slack workspace each is told in. |
| `board:read` | Reads the tasks and decisions of the projects it is placed on, to say in a message what it is about: a task's title and what changed, a decision's question and its answer leave the console in a message to someone the console told of it. |
| `events:console` | Hears HR enabled, disabled or upgraded, the people events, and the boards' events of the projects it is placed on and an agent account standing otherwise, to send them on. |
| `notifications:read` | Reads, with each of those events, whom the console told of it and under which kind, to send it on to them in Slack and to nobody else. |
| `events:plugins` | Hears HR's leave events, to tell them in Slack. |
| `jobs` | Renews Slack's configuration token, keeps the app's manifest and checks each workspace every hour; sends each person's message of the day within five minutes of their time, and tells of an agent account unheard for half an hour, or running again after that or after its usage limit; matches people every day. |
| `endpoints` | Takes what Slack sends when someone presses a shortcut or a button, verified with the signing secret, and the return of the app's installation, for whoever holds the messenger role. |
| `outbound:slack.com` | Calls Slack's Web API with the tokens the console holds: makes and updates the app, renews its token, lists members and channels, opens conversations and forms, posts and edits messages. |
| `outbound:hooks.slack.com` | Answers the person who pressed a button, through the address Slack gave that press. |
| `people:accounts`, optional | Tells the console which Slack account is which member's, as the matching by work email found it, in the workspaces whoever keeps the messenger accepted. An agent account's wake from Slack says by it which member wrote and whether one of its supervisors did, whose own word there it takes as an instruction: turn it on where the Slack workspace's addresses are held as surely as a sign-in to the console. Off, the messenger says nothing and works as before. |
| `plugins:hr`, optional | Reads HR's kinds of leave, a person's balance and their own upcoming leave, and requests or cancels leave for the person who used the form; with HR's role `hr` granted to it (the manifest's `grants`), reads each request whole, its line and its reason, for its thread. |

**HR, as the messenger reads it.** The module `src/server/integrations/hr/` is written against HR's contract ([plugins/hr/README.md](../hr/README.md), "The contract") for the versions `>=0.2.0 <0.7.0` (0.4, 0.5 and 0.6 kept 0.3's contract and moved to consoles 0.5, 0.6 and 0.6.1), and leaves any other version alone, saying so on the Connection page. It hears the eight leave events, calls `request-view` as itself and `types`, `balance`, `requests`, `request-view`, `request` and `cancel` for a person, and answers HR's decisions with the option keys `approve` and `reject`. What HR 0.3.0 added it reads where it is there and goes without where it is not: with HR 0.2, the form says no kind's rules and lists no upcoming leave to cancel. Where more than five of a person's leave may be cancelled, the form links to HR's page of their own leave, `/plugins/hr/leave` on the console.

**Board.** None: the messenger raises no decision and writes no task. It is placed on the projects whose people are sent what the console tells them, all of them unless an administrator names some when enabling it, and has no page on any ([ADR-0018](../../docs/adr/0018-the-messenger-has-no-page-on-a-project-and-the-workspace-each-is-told-in-is-said-on-its-connection-page.md)).

**Settings.** Five secrets, each sealed for `slack.com` alone: `slackConfigToken` and `slackConfigRefreshToken`, the app configuration token and its refresh token, which an administrator pastes once and the plugin renews; `slackClientSecret` and `slackSigningSecret`, sealed by the console when the plugin makes the app, the signing secret one that verifies and signs nothing; `slackBotToken`, kept per workspace (`keyed: { max: 50 }`, the workspace's id the key), sealed at each installation, forgotten when a workspace is taken away. One app, and so one of each of the others, whatever the workspaces.

**Pages.** Under Operations: Connection (the steps that connect Slack, the workspaces with their default and the way to take each away, the projects told in another than the default where there are several, how the app stands, HR, a test), Routes, People and Activity, each for the role `messenger`; My Slack notifications, for every member: which of what the console tells them reaches them in Slack, at once or daily and when, where they are told, and the way to the console's own settings of what they are told at all. No page on a project, and no widget.

### Who reads what

Every door the plugin's data leaves by ([conventions](../../docs/guide/conventions.md#who-reads-what)). `src/server/readers.test.ts` holds the records and the words to the code. Whoever keeps the messenger keeps it for every project it is on, those they may not see among them, and need not be of HR: so the role reads how the messenger runs, and nothing of what it carries. An administrator reads every record, as the console has it.

| Door | Open to | What goes through it, and why |
|---|---|---|
| Records of `apps` | an administrator alone | the app, and the states of the installation links good now, which let whoever has one install it; `status` answers what the role needs |
| Records of `connections` | an administrator alone | who connected each workspace and who installed it; `status` answers what the role needs, the projects told there by id, which says nothing of one the keeper may not see, and by name those they may |
| Records of `routes` | an administrator alone | the query `routes` answers them |
| Records of `links` | an administrator alone | who has which Slack account; the query `people` answers what the role needs |
| What the console is told of an account, with `people:accounts` | the console; through it an agent account's runner, and whoever reads the workspace's feed | which Slack account, by Slack's ids for the workspace and the account, is which member's: the console answers an agent account who wrote a message that woke it, and records each change as an event of the workspace under the messenger's name; no name, picture or address goes with it |
| Records of `threads` | an administrator alone | who asked for leave, who decides it and how it ended, which HR shows to them and to HR |
| Records of `deliveries` | an administrator alone | which task or decision an event was of, and whom one that failed had reached; `activity` answers neither |
| Records of `preferences` | one's own: `member` | what a member keeps out of Slack is theirs |
| Records of `waiting` | one's own: `member` | the words of what waits for a member's day |
| Records of `silences` | an administrator alone | which agent accounts are unheard, and whom the console told of it |
| Records of `limits` | an administrator alone | which agent accounts a usage limit stopped, and whom the console told of it |
| Words given to `connect` | kept: `app-name`, `bot-name`, `icon` | what everyone in Slack sees of the app; the messenger has no board project, so that an administrator and whoever ran a command read its event |
| The query `activity` | `messenger` | when, of which kind, how it went and why, of every project |
| The queries `status`, `people`, `routes`, `channels` | `messenger` | the connection; the members by name, who is matched and why the others are not, no address among it; the routes; the channels the app sees |
| The queries `notices`, `project-notices` | `member` | one's own choice; the workspace a project one may see is told in |

| A direct message | whom the console told of the event | what the console told them |
| A request's conversation in Slack | the person, and whoever its line asks | the request, its reason and what was answered |

## Working on it

From the repository's root, as every workspace here is worked:

```sh
pnpm install
pnpm turbo run build --filter @skillcdn/console-plugin-messenger     # dist/server/index.js and dist/web/
pnpm turbo run test --filter @skillcdn/console-plugin-messenger      # the module against a Slack, an HR and a console of the tests' own, and the package as a console reads it
pnpm --filter @skillcdn/console-plugin-messenger run check           # console-plugin check: what a console, or a store, would refuse
```

To look at its pages beside HR on a console of this machine, in the console's checkout: `pnpm dev:plugins --plugin <this repository>/plugins/hr --plugin <this repository>/plugins/messenger` ([docs/guide/local-console.md](../../docs/guide/local-console.md)). A console on this machine has no address Slack can reach, so the Connection page says so and connects nothing; trying it with Slack needs a console at a public https address and a workspace to try it in.

## Enabling it

What the administrator and whoever keeps the messenger do, which the hand-over task quotes. Read the skill first: the package ships it (`skills/messenger/SKILL.md`), and the console serves an installed plugin's skills to an administrator before it is enabled (`console plugin messenger skills`).

1. Consent to the capabilities above, each read as its sentence; turn on `plugins:hr` where HR is installed, and `people:accounts` where agent accounts are to be told in a wake from Slack which member wrote and whether a supervisor of theirs did: the console then takes a supervisor's Slack account, as matched by work email, for that supervisor's, so it is turned on by whoever answers for how the Slack workspace keeps its members' addresses. A version that asks a capability the install was not consented to waits, disabled, until an administrator consents again: coming from 0.6, that is `notifications:read`.
2. No board project. Place it on every project whose people are to be sent what the console tells them, all of them unless some are named: nothing of a project the messenger is not on reaches Slack. What each member is told at all is theirs to choose in the console's own settings, and a person who chose nothing there is told of their agent accounts alone.
3. Grant HR's role `hr` to the messenger, which the enable form offers and the plugin's page lists as what it asks of HR; without it the shortcut works and no thread is begun. Enabled before HR, the messenger is not asked again when HR comes: grant it on the plugin's page then.
4. Grant the role `messenger` to whoever keeps it.
5. At api.slack.com/apps, under "Your App Configuration Tokens", generate a token for the workspace. An administrator puts its access token in `slackConfigToken` and its refresh token in `slackConfigRefreshToken`, on the plugin's settings page or with `console plugin messenger secret <name>` at their own terminal, which takes the value typed unseen; never on a command line, through `echo` or through an agent. The other three secrets are sealed by the console from Slack's answers; nothing else is pasted.
6. Decide how the app looks in Slack before it is made, since a bot renamed once the app is installed may keep its handle: the app's name, 35 characters in any script, 콘솔 or Console unless chosen; its bot's name, in Latin letters, digits, spaces, `.`, `_` and `-`, `console` unless chosen, from which Slack makes the handle, a number added where it is taken; and an icon, the public https address of a square image of 512 to 2000 pixels, which the plugin asks Slack to fetch and, where the configuration token may not, says where to upload by hand.
7. Whoever holds `messenger`, on the Connection page or with `connect`: make the app with those names. Installing it is a person's: someone who may install apps in the Slack workspace opens the link `connect` or `install` gives, within a day, and approves; they need no account on the console. The workspace then waits on the Connection page until whoever keeps the messenger accepts it there, or with `workspace <id> --accept`, once they know it is theirs ([ADR-0016](../../docs/adr/0016-a-workspace-installed-through-a-link-waits-until-whoever-keeps-the-messenger-accepts-it.md)). Another workspace takes the app once its distribution is turned on, once, on Slack's page of the app (Manage Distribution, Activate Public Distribution), and someone there opens a link of its own; `workspace <id> --default` and `--add <project>` say which workspace each project is told in.
8. People are matched to their Slack accounts by the work email in the console's directory, in each workspace, within the hour of its installation and every day after. Whoever has none is listed by `console plugin messenger people --state no_email`, and an administrator writes one with `console person email <login> <address>`; the console writes the address of whoever signs in with Google at their next sign-in.
9. On the Routes page, the channels told of approved leave: a public channel at once, a private one once the app is invited to it in Slack.

`status` says what is left, one step at a time (`next`, with `text` in the caller's language), and the Connection page says it at its top: setting up is done when it says it is ready.

## What the deployment must provide

- A public address over https (`PUBLIC_URL`) that Slack can reach: Slack posts the presses to the endpoint `slack` and sends the installer back to `slack-oauth` under it.
- Outbound https to `slack.com` and `hooks.slack.com`.
- `AUTH_SECRET`, which the console seals the secret settings with.
- Console 0.6.2 or later, before 0.7.0, as the manifest's `console` says: the first that takes from a plugin whose an account at a messenger is (`people:accounts`); 0.6.1 was the first that tells a plugin whom it told of an event. An upgrade from data version 3 writes each member's choice in the shape of now, the kinds they turned off kept off, by the `migrate` role; a console that did not run it reads a record of before as it stands. An upgrade from a messenger before data version 3 makes the one connection of before the app and its workspace, the default; the bot token kept until then is moved under its workspace by the upgrade where the deployment gives the `migrate` role the sign-in configuration, else by the first call to Slack that needs it, or the hourly job.

Measured on a real Slack workspace since the first release: the configuration token, pasted with no expiry the console knows, renewed by the first hourly run; the form opening within the three seconds Slack allows. Not measured yet: whether an edit keeps the name and the picture a message was posted under, whether adding or taking away the shortcut asks for the app to be installed again, whether a bot renamed on an installed app gets a new handle, whether the configuration token may set the app's icon, the second form of a cancellation and its answer within the three seconds, whether closing it brings back the leave form drawn again, how long a configuration refresh token lasts, and how the moment a usage limit resets reads in a direct message, which is Slack's own date markup, to a reader in another time zone and in each language ([docs/roadmap.md](../../docs/roadmap.md), "In progress").
